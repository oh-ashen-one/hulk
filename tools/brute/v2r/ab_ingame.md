Deviations from the brief, on purpose: (1) the sprint uppercut is its own group (`uppercut`), because the combat spot has no lane to
sprint in and a run-up needs 1 s; (2) **`lunge` is an extra group**: StopLunge is not a "stop after sprint" in this game. It plays only
when jump is pressed while moving faster than RunSpeed (`AnimateClips`, `bCharging` with `Hs > RunSpeed`), so none of the six requested
groups reaches it; (3) `loco` ends with a plain stop (the game blends Run -> Walk -> Idle, no clip is involved); (4) the wall group's
three angles are back / left / back-left (a camera in front of a wall climb is inside the building).

Start positions are copied from existing cases that ran: idle `(249,150)` yaw 180 (m12_look, look_close), loco and lunge `(249,178)`
yaw -90 (v2_loco, m4), jump `(249,150)` down the same lane (its landing, y about 50, is where m4's slam crater was), combat `(255.6,190.2)`
yaw 0 (v3_combat; the old `look -25` aim at a parked car is dropped), uppercut `(259,170)` yaw 90 (m10_launcher), grab `(244.6,183.2)` yaw 180
(m2_grab_throw, same grab time 1.0), wall `(249,140)` yaw 0 (m3_wallrun; wall_run_start 1.03 s after the sprint, as measured there).
All cases: map `/Game/Maps/Manhattan`, `camDist` 7.5 (the game's initial arm length, so no transient), camPitch from the source case.
The top-level `"ab"` block in each file (film window, expected events, beats) is ignored by `LoadScript` (it reads only `spawn` and `keys`).

## The three camera angles

Same viewpoints as `ab_common.CAMERAS` (front, left side, front-left three-quarter), as the camera VIEW yaw relative to his heading
(Unreal yaw is clockwise from above): front = +180, left = +90, three-quarter = +135 (wall: back 0, left +90, back-left +45).

- **Today's schema has no camera-yaw field.** It has `spawn.camPitch`, `spawn.camDist` and a per-key `look [deg/s yaw, pitch]` rate.
  `emu` groups orbit the camera with a one-second `look` rate while he stands still (exact at 30 and 60 fps: the stop key sits 1 ms
  before the second so frame n samples the same side of it) and walk him along his original heading with a compensating stick vector
  (`mv()` in `ab_ingame.py`, derived from `WishDir`). This only works where his facing follows his velocity or nothing turns him.
- **Standing acts and carries cannot be emulated**: `StepGround` turns him toward `CamYaw` whenever he acts or holds a car while
  standing, so any camera yaw other than "behind him" is undone within about 0.4 s. `combat` and `grab` therefore use the proposed
  field `spawn.camYawOff`. Until the patch below is built into the binary a case ignores it silently (all three angles would be the
  rear view). Guards: `run` skips these cases while the source lacks the string `camYawOff`; `check` demands `camYawOff <deg>` in the capture log.

### Smallest C++ addition (not applied; proposed for the Phase 5 C++ pass)

View-only orbit; move, facing, aim, throw direction keep using `CamYaw`. About 5 lines in `unreal/WebHomage/Source/WebHomage/Brute/`:

```
BruteCharacter.h        double CamViewYawOff = 0.0;                      // next to CamDistOverride (l.159)
BruteCharacter.cpp
  LoadScript (l.244)    double CO; if ((*Sp)->TryGetNumberField(TEXT("camYawOff"), CO)) CamViewYawOff = CO;
  UpdateCamera          const FRotator Rot(Pitch, CamYaw + CamViewYawOff, 0.0);          (was CamYaw)
  UpdateCamera / Room   const FVector Bk = -FRotator(P, CamYaw + CamViewYawOff, 0.0).Vector();   (was CamYaw)
  BeginPlay log (l.108) append  " camYawOff %.0f", CamViewYawOff   (check reads it from the log)
```

With it, every group could drop the emulation (exact angles, no orbit second): change `kind` to `'patch'` in `G` and run `gen`.
Not needed for today's `emu` groups, which keep working either way.

## Gaps and risks (all unverified until the first real capture)

1. **Front angle of `grab` throws the car at the lens**: the throw aims along the gameplay camera yaw (58 m/s), and the front camera
   sits in front of him. The film window ends 0.4 s after release; the critic may see the car arrive. Smallest fix if it matters:
   `spawn.aimYawOff` (not proposed now).
2. **`loco` runs about 160 m down the lane**: ground along x=249 is proven only from y=178 to about y=90 (v2_loco) and at the leap
   landings (y=71.5, -32.6). `check` fails the case if he travels under 120 m (stuck or rescued). If it fails, shorten the sprint.
3. **Traffic**: the `(249,178)` start has cars bouncing off him at t=0-0.2 and about 2.0-2.4 (visible in v2_loco's log); the lane is
   open to cars for loco, lunge, uppercut. Same in both builds; a car between lens and body can lift the camera (the game's own
   occlusion lift), deterministic per angle. Left-side wall view runs the camera arm along the facade (about 1 m off it): the sphere
   sweep may shorten the arm; look at the first contact sheet.
4. **`grab` depends on the same parked car being in reach at t=1.0** (m2_grab_throw did). `check` fails on `grab_nothing`.
5. **Timing differs between the builds by design**: `ActHitAt` / `ActDur` come from the clips (`Config/BruteClipTiming.json`, written
   by `measure_timing.py` only for v2r clips), so contact times, car launches and impact FX move with them; the telemetry will not
   be identical, only the event order (`pack` records that in `_director/report.json`). Press spacings were chosen to survive that
   (combo presses 0.75 s apart, clap 1.7 s after the slap press, smash 1.8 s after the clap).
6. The game layers the menace posture over Idle / Walk and the sprint lean over Run in both builds; the critic judges the result.
7. Frame cadence: `-movie` uses a fixed 1/60 s step (the older non-movie cases used 1/30). Nothing here depends on that.

## GPU gates, launch budget and gpu_slot rules (do not bend them)

Why: on 2026-10-29 the Studio wedged for 7 hours: `run` made 15 cold `-game` editor launches back to back (about 34 GB each, 0 s gaps,
GPU at 100 % at every launch) while Metal/torch trainers ran that the slot tool does not count; the WindowServer watchdog killed the
session at 03:28 and the owner power-cycled at 10:16. Cold launches are allowed; unattended back-to-back launches into a busy GPU are not.

- **Machine-wide gates** (`tools/gpu/gpu_gates.py`), checked by `ab_ingame.py run` before every case AND by `tools/brute/capture.sh`
  before it asks for a slot (so a direct `capture.sh` call is gated too):
  a. an `UnrealEditor` in ps state `E` (exiting) or `Z` -> refuse: "engine stuck exiting: machine-wide pause, notify the owner".
  b. every GPU client is counted, not only Unreal: `UnrealEditor*`, `Godot*`, `Unity*` editors (not Unity Hub / shader compiler),
     `*-Shipping`, `Blender` (not blender-thumbnailer), python whose command line contains `reader.train`, `video2rig` or `torch`.
     Two or more -> refuse (ours would be the third) and print the list. A process title that is not a path counts by its first
     word (`npm exec @coding-solo/godot-mcp` is npm, not Godot).
  c. GPU `Device Utilization %` (`ioreg -r -d 1 -c IOAccelerator`) below 90 on three readings 5 s apart; otherwise poll every 15 s
     for up to 10 min, then refuse.
  d. at least 20 s since the last engine launch anywhere: `run_game.sh` stamps `~/.cache/gpu-slot/last_launch` right before it
     starts the engine (and waits out the gap there too, inside the slot); anyone else may write the same file. Waits, never refuses.
  e. unchanged: `~/.cache/gpu-slot/STREAMING` refuses (and stops `run` between cases); 11:00-13:00 ET refuses without `--ignore-window`.
  `python3 tools/gpu/gpu_gates.py status` shows what the gates see; `check` exits 3 on a refusal.
- **Frame-1 watchdog**: `capture.sh` passes `-firstline 'WH_BRUTE (tick1|t=)' -firstline_s 120` to `run_game.sh` for every Brute script
  run. No matching line within 120 s of launch -> SIGTERM our engine, wait up to 60 s, SIGKILL only if still alive, exit 5 with
  `NO FIRST FRAME`; never retried. The game logs `WH_BRUTE tick1 frame=<GFrameCounter> t=<world s>` on the Brute's first tick (log
  only). The `OriginX <= OriginMax` ensure early in every log is benign and not a failure. (An old binary without the tick1 line would
  trip the watchdog on `idle`, which has no `t=` events: rebuild first.)
- **Launch budget** in `run`: at most 6 engine launches in any rolling hour from one run, and a 30 s cool-down after each case before
  the next launch; every wait and every launch (`budget: launch n/6 this hour`) is printed. A full set of 24 cases therefore takes
  about 3.3 h of wall time for about 58 min of engine time (`run --dry-run` prints the plan). Env `AB_LAUNCHES_PER_HOUR`,
  `AB_COOLDOWN_S` exist for tests only.
- Captures go **only** through `tools/brute/capture.sh` -> `tools/gpu/gpu_slot.sh capture` (shared capture class, `-RenderOffScreen`,
  `t.MaxFPS` capped by `run_game.sh`). Never start `UnrealEditor` by hand, never open a window. One of ours at a time, strictly
  sequential; never run the old and new sets together.
- The build step (`build_brute.py`) is a headless commandlet that takes a slot through the same wrapper; the editor/game of this project
  must be closed first. Content is shared: build, capture the whole set, then build the next. Never capture during a build.
- `run` stops at the first failed case and never retries (never auto-relaunch after two crashes: after one failure read
  `<case>.log`, fix, then resume with `--cases`; finished cases are skipped). A stuck engine: stop any driver, SIGTERM the PID we started,
  wait up to 60 s, SIGKILL only if it is still alive, and do not launch another until it is gone.
- Disk is tight (APFS about 97 % full): one movie capture writes up to about 0.8 GB of PNG frames; `run` deletes them as soon as the mp4
  exists and refuses under 8 GB free. The mp4s are about 0.7 MB per game second.
- Offline test of all of the above (fake ps / ioreg, stub engine, stub capture; never launches anything):
  `tools/brute/v2r/test_ab_ingame_gates.sh`.

## GPU time

`python3 tools/brute/v2r/ab_ingame.py list` prints it. 24 captures per set, 202 game seconds. Wall time per capture is modelled as
35 s (engine start and map load: log open to first tick in the old-set logs) + 13 s per game second at 960x540 `-movie` with `t.MaxFPS 30`
(the 18 old-set captures of 2026-10-29 took 64-241 s, 16.5 s per game second including the start): about **58 min of engine time
per set**, about 3.3 h wall per set with the launch budget. A third optional set (new clips with `-WHBruteNoClipTiming`) costs the same.

## Procedure

Run from `~/hulk` on `chinese-animation-upgrades`, outside the stream window, `~/.cache/gpu-slot/STREAMING` absent
(`ls ~/.cache/gpu-slot/`), `~/.cache/gpu-slot/bin/gpu_status.sh` showing at most one other engine, editor closed.

0. One-time: apply the C++ patch above (if `combat` and `grab` are wanted), `unreal/WebHomage/Scripts/build_editor.sh`, then
   `python3 tools/brute/v2r/ab_ingame.py gen --check` (files unchanged). Both sets must use this same binary and the same `Config/`
   except for `BruteClipTiming.json` (see 2).
1. **OLD build.** Put the old set into the game: `python3 unreal/WebHomage/Scripts/build_brute.py --fbx ~/hulk-scratch/brute_v5/brute_anim.fbx`
   (the shipped one; keep a copy of that file). The capture log must show `WH_BRUTE rig: v2 clip body (... 17 clips ...)`.
2. **OLD captures**: `python3 tools/brute/v2r/ab_ingame.py run --label old --extra "-WHBruteNoClipTiming"` (constants = today's behaviour,
   whatever `Config/BruteClipTiming.json` says). Output: `~/hulk-scratch/ab-ingame/old/<case>/` (mp4, log, telemetry csv, no frames).
   Then `python3 tools/brute/v2r/ab_ingame.py check ~/hulk-scratch/ab-ingame/old` must print `ok` for every case.
3. **NEW build.** Build `brute_anim.fbx` from the v2r motion files exactly as the PLAN's Phase 5 does (assemble_motion with the new files,
   the full new set for the A/B; the keep-the-best mix is decided after this A/B), then `build_brute.py --fbx <that fbx>`.
   `Config/BruteClipTiming.json` for the new clips is the one Phase 5's C++ pass produced (`measure_timing.py`).
4. **NEW captures**: `python3 tools/brute/v2r/ab_ingame.py run --label new` (as shipped: with the timing file), then `check ... /new`.
5. **Angle sanity** (30 s, once per set; proves front / left / three-quarter really differ):
   `D=~/hulk-scratch/ab-ingame/new; ffmpeg -y -loglevel error -ss 3 -i $D/ab_idle_front/ab_idle_front.mp4 -ss 3 -i $D/ab_idle_left/ab_idle_left.mp4 -ss 3 -i $D/ab_idle_tq/ab_idle_tq.mp4 -filter_complex "[0:v][1:v][2:v]hstack=inputs=3" -frames:v 1 $D/angles_idle.png`
   and look at it (front: his face; left: his left side; tq: front-left). Same for `ab_combat_*` once the patch is in.
6. **Blind pack**: `python3 tools/brute/v2r/ab_ingame.py pack --old ~/hulk-scratch/ab-ingame/old --new ~/hulk-scratch/ab-ingame/new --out ~/hulk-scratch/ab-ingame/blind`.
   `pack` re-runs the gates (refuses failed cases without `--force`), trims each film to its film window, re-encodes both takes through
   the same ffmpeg line with metadata stripped, names them `A.mp4` / `B.mp4`, and assigns labels per case with a seeded RNG balanced to
   half old-first. It writes `blind/critic/<group>_<angle>/{A,B}.mp4` + `blind/critic/brief.md`, and, only for the director,
   `blind/_director/KEY.json` (which label is old, per case) and `report.json` (gate problems forced, event-sequence match).
7. **Critic**: `[Opus 5.5 high] opus-critic`, never skipped, given the `blind/critic` folder path and nothing else (see below).
   After its verdict only the director opens `_director/KEY.json`, then writes one row per clip into LEDGER.md
   (A/B score, winner, old/new, group cases used: Idle -> idle; Walk, Run -> loco; StopLunge -> lunge; Jump -> jump + lunge; Fall, Land -> jump;
   WallAttach, WallClimb -> wall; PunchL, PunchR, Slap, Clap, Smash -> combat; Uppercut -> uppercut; Lift, Throw -> grab).
   A clip ships only if it wins or ties (PLAN).
8. Cleanup after the verdict: leave the game's Content on the set the director decides to ship; `~/hulk-scratch/ab-ingame/` is small
   (mp4s) and uncommitted; remove it when the ledger rows are written.

## What the critic is given, and what it is not

Given: the folder `blind/critic/` with `brief.md` and 24 folders `<group>_<angle>/{A.mp4,B.mp4}` (960x540, 60 fps, trimmed to the film
window; each move from three fixed angles; the brief lists the clips each folder group exercises and the script's beat times).
It scores per clip (weight, readability, contact, foot contact, arm arc and mesh, transitions, menace, 1-5) and names a winner or a tie.

Not given: `_director/` (KEY, report), the telemetry csv and logs (they name the clip timing file and paths), the build or file names,
which take is old or new, how either was made, the Blender A/B results or any numbers from them, the expected outcome. The brief says only that the
takes differ in the Brute's animation clips. The director does not discuss the pair with the critic before the verdict.
